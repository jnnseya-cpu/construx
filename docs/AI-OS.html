How to read this

Every claim in this document carries a status. A specification that does not distinguish what runs from what is intended is a document that gets a team to build the wrong half.

Built in the repository now, covered by tests
Partial core built, the gap named
Spec designed here, not implemented
Bounded deliberately constrained — see below

The figures throughout are measured from the codebase, not estimated. Where this document specifies something new it says so, and where the platform already answers a requirement it names the module that answers it.

One deviation, stated once

The brief calls for a platform that operates “without requiring human intervention”, including agents that patch code and redeploy services on their own. CONSTRUX will not do that, and the constraint is load-bearing rather than timid.

This platform exists to produce a record that survives adjudication. Its event catalogue marks governance decisions aiAllowed: false, and a commit by an AI actor against one of those types is a hard failure in the ledger — not a policy, a refusal. An agent that could approve a payment certificate, sign off a permit or redeploy the service that holds the evidence would destroy the one property the product is bought for.

So autonomy here is bounded and graded, not absent. The mandate ladder in §07 gives agents real operating latitude — continuous observation, drafting, proposing, and unattended execution inside an explicitly granted envelope — while keeping the acts that create legal or financial liability attached to a named person. That is the same posture Palantir takes with analyst-in-the-loop workflows and the same one every regulated trading system takes with pre-trade limits: the system is autonomous inside a boundary somebody signed.

02

Executive product vision

What it is, what it replaces, and why the record is the moat.

CONSTRUX is the operating system for the delivery of physical assets. One data model and one operating logic carry a project from the first feasibility option through tender, design, construction, commissioning, handover and thirty years of operation — without a migration, an export, or a second system of record.

The problem it solves

Construction loses money in the gaps between systems. The estimate lives in a spreadsheet, the programme in scheduling software, the correspondence in an inbox, the quality records in a folder, and the evidence on somebody's phone. When a claim is argued three years later, the cost of proving what happened exceeds the value of the claim — so it is settled rather than defended. The industry calls this a documentation problem. It is a provenance problem, and it is worth billions a year.

What makes it different

Every state change in CONSTRUX is an event in an append-only, hash-chained ledger. The chain detects deletion and reordering, not merely alteration. Replay reconstructs the project at any moment and reports, per event, whether it verifies. That is not an audit feature bolted to a CRUD application; it is the storage layer, and every module is built on it.

The commercial consequence is the moat. A competitor can copy a screen in a quarter. They cannot retrospectively produce a verifiable chain for a project that has already been built, and a customer three years into a chain cannot leave without abandoning the thing that defends their claims. Switching cost compounds with tenure.

Why the market needs it now

  • Regulation moved. The UK Building Safety Act made the golden thread a statutory duty for higher-risk buildings, with personal liability attached. Comparable regimes are arriving across the EU and Gulf. Compliance is no longer a differentiator; it is a licence to operate.
  • AI made extraction cheap and trust expensive. Anybody can now read a drawing with a model. Almost nobody can prove which model read it, against which revision, at what confidence, and who accepted the answer. CONSTRUX records all four on every AI write.
  • The buyer changed. Margin pressure moved procurement authority from IT to the commercial director, who buys instruments that defend money, not platforms that store documents.

The AI-OS thesis

The platform is not a product with AI features. It is an operating system in the literal sense: it schedules work across a fleet of specialist agents, arbitrates their access to resources through a capability model, meters and bills their consumption, isolates their tenancies, and refuses their unsafe instructions. The agents are processes; the ledger is the filesystem; the permission matrix is the kernel's access control; the ACU wallet is the scheduler's quota.

Technical weaknesses found

WeaknessConsequenceRemedy
Single-process ledger No horizontal scale. Availability is bounded by one container; a restart is a cold start with a full replay. §14 — the verified Postgres schema plus a wire-protocol client. The schema enforces append-only, RLS and chain integrity under concurrency and is proven against a live server; the driver is the missing piece.
Replay is O(n) from genesis Restore time grows without bound. A ten-year project becomes a multi-minute boot. §14 — periodic signed snapshots, replay from the last verified snapshot forward, snapshot hash chained into the log so it cannot be forged.
No semantic retrieval The document index is lexical. It finds revision C beside revision B; it cannot find the clause expressed in other words. §10 — an embedding capability routed through the existing orchestrator, with the same refusal discipline as perception.
No log or metric egress Structured JSON goes to stdout and nothing collects it. Post-incident analysis depends on container retention. §14 — OTLP exporter behind the existing telemetry interface. The counters and the audit stream already exist; only the shipper is missing.
Rate limiting is per-instance without Redis N replicas enforce the limit N times over. Already solved but unconfigured: a shared limiter exists and is verified against a fake Redis. This is a deployment gap, not a code gap.

Commercial weaknesses found

  • Revenue is subscription-plus-AI only. The platform sits directly on the payment path between main contractor and supply chain — the largest cash flow in the industry — and takes nothing from it. §11 and §13 close this.
  • The data asset is not monetised. Cost intelligence is built from committed records per tenancy and never aggregated. An anonymised benchmark across tenancies is a second product with near-zero marginal cost — and one that must be built with explicit consent and k-anonymity, not assumed.
  • No self-serve expansion. ACU top-ups exist; there is no upsell engine, no usage-triggered plan recommendation, and no churn signal derived from the very engagement data the ledger already holds.

06

AI command centres

One per user type, assembled from the same seven functions. What differs is the authority it inherits and the questions it can answer.

A command centre is not a chat window. It is a working surface with four fixed regions, and the platform already answers all four for every screen: what is happening, what changed, what is at risk, and what to do next. The AI layer populates them from the ledger rather than from a prompt.

The rule that makes it safe

A command centre inherits the authority of the person viewing it and never exceeds it. It cannot read a record its viewer could not open, and it cannot offer an action its viewer could not perform. This is enforced by passing the ordinary engine context — the same one every domain command authorises against — rather than by filtering results after the fact.

07

The agent workforce and its mandate ladder

Twelve agents run today. The specification takes it to thirty-one — and every one of them declares, in code, exactly how far it may go alone.

The mandate ladder

Every agent carries a mandate: the capability areas it may read, the areas it may propose in, the roles that may approve its proposals, and its unattended ceiling. The ceiling is the load-bearing field.

OBSERVE

Reads and reports. Produces findings, never a command. Today's ceiling for the radar, pipeline, HSEQ and supply-chain agents.

DRAFT

Prepares a complete, valid command and holds it. Nothing reaches the ledger. This is what the perception drafts already do.

PROPOSE

Places the command in the approval queue scoped to the capability area, where a named role decides. Today's ceiling for the other eight agents, and the highest any agent currently reaches.

ACT

Executes unattended inside an explicitly granted envelope: named commands, a value ceiling, a time window, and a revocation that takes effect on the next tick. Exists in the model; granting it is a deliberate product decision, and a test fails if an agent grants it to itself.

Where ACT may be granted, and where it may not

Eligible: acts that are reversible, bounded in value, and create no liability — refreshing a forecast, re-running a clash detection, drafting a transmittal, opening a low-value enquiry, closing a duplicate observation.

Never eligible, enforced by the catalogue: anything marked aiAllowed: false — certifying payment, approving a variation, signing a gate decision, issuing a statutory notice, closing an NCR, accepting handover, granting a role. A commit by an AI actor against one of these types is a hard failure in commit(), not a rejected request.

Agent execution contract

type AgentMandate = {
  reads:        CapabilityArea[];   // may read, nothing wider
  proposes:     CapabilityArea[];   // may propose in, subset of reads
  approvers:    Role[];             // who may decide
  maxUnattended: 'OBSERVE' | 'DRAFT' | 'PROPOSE' | 'ACT';
  envelope?: {                      // required when maxUnattended is ACT
    commands:    string[];          // an allow-list, never a pattern
    valueCapMinor: number;          // 0 for non-financial acts
    window:      { from: string; to: string };
    revocable:   true;              // always; effective next tick
  };
};

Every agent run produces a run report — findings, proposals raised, ACUs consumed — and every proposal expires. An unattended proposal that nobody decides is withdrawn after 72 hours rather than executing by default, because a queue that drains itself by timeout is an agent acting unattended by another name.

The self-managing platform layer

Self-healing where the blast radius is bounded, and a loud refusal where it is not.

CapabilityBehaviourStatus
Health watch Six rules over the gateway counters every interval, as rates over the window rather than totals since boot. Fires on transition in, re-notifies after an interval, and reports recovery. Declines to judge below a minimum sample. Built
Writer protection A second process on the same journal volume refuses to start. Heartbeat rather than pid, because two containers have separate pid namespaces and the naive check takes the lock. Built
Delivery guarantee Transactional outbox with backoff and abandonment. A process that dies mid-send leaves the notice owed, not lost. Built
AI failure containment Provider health, fallback to a healthy provider, refusal on an empty wallet, and no charge without a ledger write. Built
Chain self-verification Scheduled background replay of a sample of projects, verifying chain and state hashes and raising a critical alert on the first divergence — rather than discovering it during a dispute. Spec
Defect triage Clusters errors by route and correlation id, attaches the request shape and the trace, opens one finding rather than a thousand log lines. Spec
Auto-repair Bounded to restart and reroute. Restarting a stuck timer, re-establishing a provider connection, draining a wedged queue, failing over a connector. Spec
Autonomous code patching and redeploy Refused. An agent that can change the code that holds the evidence can change the evidence. The release agent proposes a change with its test evidence and a human merges. Bounded
Why auto-repair stops at restart

The industry precedent for autonomous remediation — Netflix, Google SRE — is reverting to a known-good state, not authoring a fix. Rollback, restart, drain, shed load, fail over. Every one of those returns the system to something a human already approved. Generating and deploying a patch does not, and on a platform whose product is an evidential record it converts an availability incident into an integrity incident.

Data and intelligence layer

The ledger is the source of truth. Everything else is a derived read model that can be rebuilt from it and is therefore allowed to be lost.

The architectural rule

No store other than the event log is authoritative. A warehouse, a vector index or a graph that disagrees with the ledger is wrong by definition and is rebuilt, not reconciled. This is what keeps a lakehouse from quietly becoming a second system of record with different numbers in it.

Learning that is allowed, and learning that is not

The platform improves in three ways, and none of them is silent model drift on customer data.

  • Measured accuracy per task. The evaluation harness scores accounting, boundary, refusal, injection and determined cases against a gold set with hand-derived expected values. Routing decisions use these numbers.
  • Disposition feedback. Every AI output records whether a person accepted it, accepted it with changes, or rejected it — and the corrections are stored separately from the model's original answer. That is a labelled dataset produced as a by-product of normal work.
  • Cost intelligence from committed records. Rates derived from what was actually paid, not from a library. Cross-tenant aggregation requires explicit consent and k-anonymity thresholds, and is a separate product with a separate agreement.
Not permitted

Training or fine-tuning on customer data without an explicit, revocable, per-tenancy agreement. Provider clearance levels already prevent privileged material reaching an under-cleared vendor; the same discipline governs any training corpus.

BitriPay integration gateway

A payments layer on the largest cash flow in construction, built as an adapter behind a provider-neutral interface.

What this section does and does not specify

This specifies the contract CONSTRUX needs from a payment provider and the whole surface around it — merchant onboarding, key management, webhooks, settlement, reconciliation, disputes and revenue share. It deliberately does not invent BitriPay's endpoint paths, field names, signature scheme or error codes. Those must come from BitriPay's own published specification, and writing plausible ones here would produce a document an engineer would implement and then have to unpick.

The adapter interface below is the integration point. Mapping it to BitriPay's actual API is a bounded task — a day's work against real documentation — and the same interface admits Stripe, Adyen and Checkout.com without touching anything above it.

Why payments belong in this platform

CONSTRUX already runs the payment cycle: application, certification, payment notice, pay-less notice, settlement — with the statutory dates of the Construction Act enforced and the applicant structurally unable to certify their own claim. What it does not do is move the money. That gap is a lost revenue line and, more importantly, a lost reconciliation: the platform knows exactly what should have been paid, and has no record of what was.

The adapter interface

type PaymentProvider = {
  readonly name: string;
  readonly capabilities: PaymentMethod[];   // QR | WALLET | CARD | BANK_TRANSFER | MOBILE_MONEY
  readonly settlementCurrency: string[];

  /** Idempotent by construction: the key is the certificate id. */
  createPayment(request: {
    idempotencyKey: string;
    amountMinor: number;
    currency: string;
    method: PaymentMethod;
    reference: string;         // the certificate reference, carried end to end
    payer: PartyRef;
    payee: PartyRef;
    split?: RevenueSplit[];    // platform fee and any partner share
  }): Promise;

  capture(intentId: string): Promise;
  refund(paymentId: string, amountMinor: number, reason: string): Promise;
  settlementReport(from: string, to: string): Promise;

  /** Verifies the provider's signature. Returns undefined on any failure —
      an unverifiable webhook is discarded, never processed optimistically. */
  verifyWebhook(headers: Headers, rawBody: Buffer): WebhookEvent | undefined;
};

The path to horizontal scale

  1. Snapshotting — periodic signed state snapshots with the snapshot hash chained into the log. Restore replays from the last verified snapshot forward, turning an O(n) boot into an O(recent) one. Spec
  2. The Postgres client — the schema is written and proven against a live Postgres 16: append-only rules, forced RLS, a chain-head row lock plus a unique index so two concurrent writers produce exactly one winner, and an evidence trigger. What is missing is a wire-protocol client, because zero runtime dependencies is settled. Writing one is a bounded, testable piece of work. Partial
  3. Read replicas — derived positions and the console's read path move to replicas; the write path stays on the primary, where the chain trigger serialises it. Spec
  4. Stateless application tier — with the ledger in Postgres and the limiter in a shared store, the application becomes horizontally scalable and the writer lock becomes redundant rather than load-bearing. Spec

Enforced by the database, not by the application

  • Append-only — DO INSTEAD NOTHING rules on UPDATE and DELETE. Not a revoked grant: a grant can be given back, and “the application cannot update” is weaker than “the table cannot be updated”.
  • Tenant isolation — RLS keyed on a session variable and FORCEd so it binds the owner too.
  • Chain integrity under concurrency — a row lock on the project head plus the unique index. Two writers both try; exactly one wins.
  • Evidence — a trigger against the list of event types that require it.
A finding worth carrying into any deployment

The verification script's first run passed every isolation check while the database was enforcing nothing. It connected as the bootstrap superuser, and row-level security does not apply to a superuser — nor to any role holding BYPASSRLS — whatever FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY says. Each tenancy read the other's events and the cross-tenant write succeeded.

The policies were right and the connection was wrong. That is exactly the mistake a deployment makes and exactly the one a schema file cannot show. The application role is NOSUPERUSER NOBYPASSRLS, and one of the checks asserts it is neither.

API specification

705 routes are published today, each with the schema that governs its body. The additions follow the conventions the platform already enforces.

Conventions, enforced by tests

  • Every write route declares a JSON Schema. A route without one fails the suite.
  • Every write route is reachable from the console — a capability with no door is a capability nobody has.
  • Every registered event type has a command that emits it. An orphan event fails the suite.
  • Errors are RFC 7807 application/problem+json, with field-level detail where a schema rejected the body.
  • Every response carries x-correlation-id; every log line carries the trace and the authorisation decision.
  • Writes answer 201. A denial is a denial — never an empty list.

Competitive advantage

Five properties a competitor cannot reach by shipping features.

  1. The chain cannot be retrofitted. A competitor can add an audit log tomorrow. They cannot produce a verifiable chain for a project that finished last year, and the customer three years in cannot leave without abandoning the thing that defends their claims.
  2. AI provenance is a moat, not a feature. Every AI write carries the provider, the model class, the confidence, the prompt version, the assumptions, the known gaps and the human disposition. A platform that cannot say which model produced a number cannot sell into a regulated golden thread at any price.
  3. Refusal is a product property. The platform refuses to read a drawing with a model that cannot see, refuses to record an unscanned file as checked, refuses to file a commitment the letter does not contain, refuses to boot on a broken chain. Every refusal is a place a competitor will ship a plausible answer — and be found out once.
  4. One code path from sole trader to multi-billion. Thresholds scale; logic does not fork. The SME entry price and the enterprise contract are the same product, so the land-and-expand motion has no re-platforming cliff in it.
  5. Payments close the loop. The platform already knows what should be paid and enforces the statutory dates that govern it. Moving the money makes it the system of record for the transaction as well as the entitlement — and that is the position nobody displaces.

The one-line assessment

CONSTRUX is production-ready as a single-instance enterprise deployment with a stated availability SLA, and is not yet ready as a multi-tenant public platform at scale. The distance between those two positions is the Postgres client, the observability egress and the object-storage adapter — three bounded, testable pieces of work, all of them specified above, none of them research.
